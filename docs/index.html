JobsDoctor (working title)

Resuscitate your job search.

An AI career clinic. A short intake with our AI nurse helps you put into words what you actually want from your next role, not just which title you think fits. Then the doctor writes your job prescription: your diagnosis and guiding principle, areas to target with real roles and pay, questions to ask employers, how to get the meeting, and a ready-to-use pitch.

Prototype

Reachable user

Business students in my program who are actively looking for internships or first jobs, and all of them use LinkedIn. Many aren't sure yet which roles fit them, what they pay, or where to start.

Before / after workflow

Before

  • Unsure which roles or industries fit
  • Mass-apply to dozens of jobs and hope something sticks
  • Wait weeks for a career counselor appointment
  • Piece together generic advice from the internet

After (JobsDoctor)

  • A short AI intake that helps you name what you really want
  • Get a personal plan: areas to target, roles and pay, what to ask, and what to say
  • Apply to a few roles that truly fit
  • Come back to your coach anytime

One core workflow

  1. User enters a first name to start.
  2. The AI intake nurse asks where they are, what they're drawn to and what they've done, then two reflective questions; she reflects back the criterion behind the job title and the user confirms or corrects it, then she covers location, pay and deal-breakers.
  3. The app builds a Career Plan dashboard: guiding principle, the areas to target (what each is, why it fits, where to look) and the specific roles within each, with pay estimates, questions to ask, how to get the meeting, a pitch, and next steps.

Biggest risk

That a short AI conversation can't produce a plan specific and trustworthy enough for a job seeker to act on, so people would get just as much from asking a general chatbot.

How this build tests it

  • A real Claude-powered run, end to end: six questions, then a full Career Plan dashboard.
  • The dashboard follows a fixed coaching structure (guiding principle, areas to target with specific roles and pay, questions to ask, how to get the meeting, pitch, next steps) that a general chatbot doesn't give you by default.
  • PostHog tracks how many people who start the chat reach a finished plan, where they drop off, and whether they copy their pitch or check off next steps.
  • Pod testers answer one question afterward: "Would you act on this plan this week?"

How the app works

  1. The user clicks Get Started, checks in with a first name, and chats with the intake nurse in the browser.
  2. Each answer goes to a small JobsDoctor server running on my computer. The server holds the API key (in a private .env file) and the coaching playbook, so neither is visible in the browser or on GitHub.
  3. The server sends the conversation to Claude (Sonnet 5.5) with instructions for each of eight intake steps; the app adds a final "anything else?" question. While the prescription is written, the waiting room shows the nurse, a heart monitor and an Rx pad.
  4. The server then asks Claude to return the Career Plan in a fixed structure, and the app renders it as a dashboard the user can check off, copy from, or save as a PDF.

Cost per run

About 2–3 cents per completed run: six Claude calls on Sonnet 5.5, priced at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. The server prints the exact cost of every call in the Terminal.

Non-goals (for now)

  • No social feed. No posts, likes, or popularity contest. Users keep LinkedIn for that.
  • No mass-apply. No auto-apply bots; JobsDoctor helps you choose, not spam.
  • No public AI access yet. The AI runs on my computer to keep the API key private; a hosted version comes next.

Lessons
  • An API key can't go in a public repo or web page: GitHub scans public repos, and exposed Anthropic keys get disabled automatically. The key has to live on a server.
  • Keeping the coaching prompts on the server also protects the product's structure, which is part of what makes it different from a general chatbot.
  • Asking the AI for a fixed JSON structure makes the plan consistent enough to render as a dashboard.
  • The AI job-search space is crowded, but most tools push mass-applying. Coaching people toward fewer, better-fit applications is an open lane.
Problems
  • GitHub Pages can only serve files, so the AI can't run on the public site yet. For now it runs on my computer; a hosted version (e.g., a Cloudflare Worker) is next.
  • AI pay ranges are estimates and can be off, so the dashboard labels them and tells users to verify pay and deadlines.
